Subject: Revised legal basis: controllership and the research basis (DBL §10)

Hi Jade,

Our earlier consultations ran on an unverified premise: that we students are the controllers. That premise does not hold. The processing is part of Daniel Hardt's research line: he supervised the original BSc thesis, proposed continuing the research beyond it, co-authored the resulting paper, and funded its presentation at SLTC 2024 from his research funds. **Under Datatilsynet's role guidance (Rollefordeling i forskningsprojekter, Jul 2023, §3.1.7), a student working within a supervisor's project acts on behalf of the university. The university is therefore the sole controller (Art. 4(7)), and we process under its authority (Art. 29).**

Legal basis
- Art. 6(1)(e), public-interest task (university research mandate): general basis.
- Art. 9(2)(j) + DBL §10, special categories: processable without consent for research of "væsentlig samfundsmæssig betydning" to which the processing is necessary.
  - Significance: the work extends Daniel Hardt's research line on LLM-proxy survey respondents; our peer-reviewed abstract "LLMs as Proxy Survey Participants With RAG" (Torjani, Brikas & Hardt) was accepted at SLTC 2024 and addresses individual-level LLM imitation, a recognised open frontier.
  - Necessity: Measuring how faithfully an LLM reproduces an individual's survey responses from that individual's own chat data requires the authentic corpus. Synthetic personas or demographic prompts remove precisely the signal under test.
- SUBJECTs consent regardless (Art. 6(1)(a)/9(2)(a)), and we have already received consent from all of our subjects.
- OTHERs and mentioned persons are covered by the research regime above; no consent is required, and none is obtainable, since donors are the only channel to their contacts.

Notice (Art. 14(5)(b)) – Individual notice to 3rd party chat partners is exempt on the grounds of disproportionate effort. 
There is no delivery channel: no API for personal Messenger, Instagram, or WhatsApp, and the donor is the only conduit. Notice would mean every donor manually messaging 100–1000 private contacts. The effort falls entirely on donors, with real social cost, which leaves the donor cohort effectively unobtainable. 

We therefore rely on (i) the safeguards required by Arts. 89(1) and 32 GDPR (pseudonymisation, data minimisation, access control, retention period); (ii) a donor-facing T&C as substitute transparency measure under Art. 14(5)(b); and (iii) a mechanism for data subjects to request erasure under Art. 17.

**We plan to proceed on this basis, but welcome comments on any specific defect you identify in the basis or safeguards.**

Best regards,
